Abstract

Narrative video understanding requires more than recognizing visible events: models must acquire dialogue evidence, bind it to speakers, and use it to infer plot meaning. Existing video benchmarks evaluate pieces of this problem, including video question answering, audio-visual reasoning, and temporal grounding, but rarely test whether these abilities form a coherent dialogue-to-plot evidence chain within the same clips.

We introduce CineMME, a human-verified benchmark designed to diagnose dialogue-grounded plot understanding, supported by approximately 1,600 expert person-hours of annotation. CineMME contains 975 curated short narrative video clips and two linked tracks: CineMME-Grounding, with 13,554 timestamped speech segments and 33,083 speaker face boxes for temporal dialogue localization, transcription, and active-speaker grounding; and CineMME-Reasoning, with 1,600 multiple-choice and 316 open-ended questions spanning perception, audio, narrative, social, and scene-presentation understanding.

Our main evaluation compares 20 MLLMs on reasoning and 9 omni-modal models on grounding. The results reveal a pronounced who-what-when decoupling: Gemini-3 Pro localizes dialogue in time (68.34% tIoU) and partially transcribes it (WER 28.99%), yet reaches only 7.74% vIoU for active-speaker grounding while achieving 67.66% MCQ accuracy over extractable answers. Modality ablations further show that vision is not uniformly beneficial: it improves perception-heavy questions but can hurt dialogue-centric reasoning. CineMME provides a compact diagnostic testbed for evaluating how multimodal video models connect dialogue, speakers, visual evidence, and narrative meaning.

Benchmark

Dataset statistics

Short speech segments demand precise timing, and tiny, small, and medium face boxes make speaker grounding hard in multi-person scenes. Questions are spread across five categories, and 76.1% of them exceed 20 words.
